Prospects of Using Spirulina in Poultry Farming

Project Status: Implementation Stage

Open presentation in PDF

Dear colleagues, partners, domestic producers, and all those interested in creating and implementing modern technologies in Ukraine!

At the request of Ukrainian agribusiness representatives to inform them about innovative technologies in agribusiness, we have prepared information about an innovative product - a bioactive diluted feed material from spirulina, specially developed for broilers and laying hens, taking into account modern European requirements for poultry products. Currently, our team of scientists is actively working with UNERA Luxembourg S.A., an international leader in biotechnology development, so the information is reliable and credible.

What is the essence of the innovation?

At first glance, spirulina is a well-known topic. But in practice, its use in commercial poultry farming was almost impossible due to a dense cell wall rich in polysaccharides, which complicates the absorption of nutrients. Moreover, traditional spirulina has large particles that are difficult to digest, and its addition to feed is inefficient and expensive. This problem was solved jointly with UNERA Luxembourg S.A., which applied a patented "open cell" technology. This is a unique method of processing spirulina in controlled magnetic fields, without heating, acids or alkalis, which allows:

— Carefully destroy the outer cell wall
— Preserve all bioactive components in their natural form and make them fully available to the organism.

Thanks to the collaboration, a diluted product was obtained that is administered through the water supply system, without preservatives, additives, or changes in the diet or additional feed costs. Essentially, it is a new generation natural phytobiotic. It contains phycocyanin, vitamins A, B-complex, D3, K1, amino acids, minerals, omega-3, 6 and 9, carotenoids and antioxidants. All these substances:

— Support immunity and gut microflora;
— Reduce the need for antibiotics
— Improve digestion and feed absorption.

This resulted in:

— Increased profitability per bird
— Reduced feed conversion rate
— Reduced mortality by 8–10%
— Greater live weight with the same amount of feed
— Reduced ammonia emissions and litter contamination
— Improved egg quality.

Moreover, in one study where a group of birds was infected, the application of spirulina along with the basic treatment regimen gave better results than standard antibiotic therapy.

Thus, the technology of diluted spirulina is not feed or medicine, but an intelligent bioactive solution that optimizes growth, reduces losses, reduces the need for antibiotics, and simultaneously increases the profitability of poultry farming.

We hope that the implementation of this innovative technology will enable Ukrainian agribusiness to produce healthy food for Ukrainians and be competitive in international markets.
