Prospects of Titanium Production Technologies

Project Status: Ready for Implementation

Criteria of Efficiency:

— Quality: control of impurity content, alloying, structural homogeneity
— Cost: process optimization, raw material availability, energy efficiency.

Oxygen Control — Key Parameter

— Oxygen as an alloying element: at concentrations up to 0.1% — increases ductility; above 0.7% — leads to its loss
— Modern production achieves oxygen content of 0.02–0.04%, providing optimal mechanical properties
— Some standards (e.g., Grade 4 ASTM B 348-83) allow content up to 0.4%.

Titanium Production Technologies:

1. Chloride Technology (Magnesium Reduction)

— Purification of titanium tetrachloride through multi-stage chemical rectification
— High-precision alloying of titanium sponge with oxygen in reactive gas mixture Ar + O₂
— Automatic supply of mixture under controlled pressure (< 0.04 kgf/cm²)
— Alternative method: dosed introduction of oxygen in the form of water vapor.

2. Melting of Undisintegrated Blocks

— Developed electron beam technology for melting titanium blocks weighing up to 2.7 tons
— Elimination of sponge crushing and sorting operations — increased efficiency and reduced losses.

3. Direct Recovery Methods from TiO₂:

3.1 Metallothermic Reduction:

— Ca: TiO₂ + 2Ca → Ti + 2CaO at 1050 °C
— CaH₂: TiO₂ + 2CaH₂ → Ti + 2CaO + 2H₂ at 1200 °C.

3.2 Titanium Powder with Particles <100 μm, Bulk Density 0.6–0.8 g/cm³

3.3 High Purity and Controlled Residual Calcium Content (<0.05%).

4. Raw Material Purification: Opening of Ilmenite Concentrates

Obtaining Technical TiO₂ through Intermediate Complex Compounds:

— Elimination of energy-intensive titanium slag smelting
— Complete regeneration of complexing agent and recycling
— Processing of ilmenites with TiO₂ content from 42%
— Deep purification and obtaining titanium dioxide with purity up to 99%+
— Comprehensive extraction of accompanying elements: Zr, Fe, Cr, Ni, Co, V, Sc, Li, etc.

Conclusions

Regulation of titanium's physical-mechanical properties is possible within existing industrial technological schemes through controlled alloying.

Powder metallurgy and direct recovery methods provide the basis for additive manufacturing of products for critical applications.

The progress of titanium technologies is determined by the balance of "quality - cost", which is particularly relevant for aerospace, energy, and medical industries.
