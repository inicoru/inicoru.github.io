Environmental Monitoring
Under Increased Electromagnetic Impact

Project Status: Research Stage

Project Objective

Development and application of Earth remote sensing technology (ERS) to detect the impact of electromagnetic radiation (EMR) concentrations on the biosphere, infrastructure, and ecosystems.

Partners: Special Center for Aerospace Technologies, NDI "MIP" (Ukraine).

Technical Foundation

Imaging is performed in the infrared and red spectrum ranges, allowing:

— Register thermal signatures of the Earth's surface
— Assess the state of air, water, and biological resources
— Operate at local, regional, and global levels.

Monitoring is based on electronic observation systems connected to base stations via:

— Cellular networks
— Aerial and satellite platforms with multispectral sensors.

Types of Remote Sensing:

1. Passive

— Registration of natural radiation (solar or intrinsic)
— Sensors cover from ultraviolet to far infrared
— Used for: vegetation analysis (e.g., chlorophyll content), water and air pollution assessment, soil disturbance detection.

2. Semi-active

— Use of reflected sunlight
— Receiver and source are separated in space
— Instruments: spectrometers, cameras, scanners.

3. Active

— Own radiation (e.g., LIDAR)
— Allows obtaining topographic information in inaccessible regions
— Used for risk prediction, including infrastructure collapses.

Areas of Technology Application:

1. Environmental Monitoring

— Assessment of air, water, and soil quality
— Detection of pollution, sources of toxic emissions, discharges, and erosion
— Determination of maximum permissible concentrations (MPC) of harmful substances.

2. Solid Waste Monitoring

— Detection of toxic components
— Determination of penetration depth into soil
— Prediction of the moment when toxic components reach groundwater level
— Analysis of air above landfills for exceeding permissible concentrations.

3. Hazardous Chemical Facilities

— Monitoring of tanks, pipelines, and storage facilities
— Detection of chemical contamination threats
— Early warning of accidents or leaks.

4. Geological Safety of Buildings and Structures

— Radio and thermal imaging for: detecting cracks, landslides, karst cavities; predicting collapses; preventing sudden destruction due to geodynamic processes.

Electromagnetic Impact Control

Continuous monitoring of EMF levels allows:

— Identify areas exceeding permissible EMF levels
— Predict risks to human health, plants, and animals
— Prevent failures in power systems
— Conduct environmental audits after accidents at energy facilities.

Analytics and Visualization

— Results are presented in the form of thematic maps, models, and state indices
— Calculation of object state indicator:
P = f(D, x₁, x₂, x₃...), where D is the reference state, xₙ are actual parameters fixed by remote sensing.

Conclusion

Systematic remote sensing allows:

— Ensure environmental safety of territories
— Predict risks of natural and man-made character
— Control EM-radiation as a source of potential danger to the environment and population.

Monitoring is not just observation, but a tool for prediction and prevention.
